One fixed camera over a Tashkent junction. Every violation as a time segment.

Part A turns a video into [start_sec, end_sec, label] segments. Part B gives a causal risk, frame by frame, that an accident starts within 5 seconds. Detector and tracker are open weights; the scene (roadway, crossings, stop line, traffic light, solid lines) was drawn once by hand and is re-aligned to every clip automatically.

Live demo

Upload a clip, get the events back

Accepts an .mp4 from this camera; the first 3 minutes are analysed (about 500 MB max). It runs on a free CPU, so a 2-minute clip takes a few minutes; a progress bar shows the stage. You get the annotated video, a timeline with the traffic-light row, the Part B risk curve and the raw detect_events() output. A 12-second example clip is one click away.

Exploratory analysis

What the four clips told us, and what it changed

Findings that shaped the solution

framing
The clips are not pixel-identical. Against the clip we drew the scene on, the others are shifted by 11–30 px (720p). A fixed polygon would put the stop line in the wrong lane, so every clip's median background is matched to a reference frame (SIFT + RANSAC) and the scene is moved before any rule runs.
signal
In daylight the lamp is not the brightest thing. The sunlit housing outshines a lit lamp, so brightness read "red" for five minutes straight. Colour works: red lamp R ≫ G, B; green lamp G ≫ R. Measured over complete phases: red 28–39 s, green 32–41 s including the blinking green and amber.
perspective
Boxes of queued cars overlap all the time. The camera looks down at a steep angle, so neighbours in a queue overlap in the image. Every box-contact "accident" we inspected was a queue. We dropped accident / near-miss from Part A and rebuilt Part B around steady closing on a collision course.
false people
The detector sees people that are not there. A traffic-light head was tracked as a "person" for 30 s; couriers on bikes count as pedestrians. Static "people" and riders are removed before any pedestrian rule, and regions are tested at the feet, not the box centre.
two-way road
There is no single main direction. The median splits two opposite flows, so "against the median flow" flagged half of all cars as wrong-way. The flow map below learns the direction per grid cell instead.
crowds
Pedestrians do not walk inside the paint. In a crowd people step a metre off the zebra. We drew crossings about half a zebra wider on each side and marked refuge islands as places where a pedestrian may stand.

Learned (open weights, no fine-tuning)

  • YOLOv8n trained on COCO (Ultralytics, AGPL-3.0): cars, buses, trucks, motorcycles, bicycles, people.
  • ByteTrack for identities across frames.
  • No other training data. No hosted APIs at inference. Weights: 6 MB, shipped in weights/.

Hand-made, drawn once for this camera

  • Roadway, crossings and refuge islands, the stop line with the light that controls it, solid lane lines. Stored in src/scene.json, drawn on src/scene_ref.jpg with our browser scene editor.
  • Per-clip alignment and the signal reader are classical computer vision, not learned.

Not emitted in Part A: accident, near_miss, illegal_turn, illegal_u_turn, road_obstacle, fire_smoke. A class we predict that is absent from the test set adds a zero to the macro average, so we only fire classes we could verify on the samples.

Part B: accident anticipation

A light online tracker runs on every 5th frame. A pair of vehicles raises the risk only when their gap shrank on each of the last four updates, their straight-line paths bring the boxes into contact within 1.5 s, and the hazard persists for about 0.7 s. Pairs already overlapping (a queue seen at an angle) are ignored. On the samples the risk stays near 0 and crosses 0.5 on about 2–3 % of frames. step() sees frames in order and nothing else.

Report

What worked, what did not, what is next

Worked

  • Drawing the scene once and aligning it per clip: the stop line lands on the right lanes in all four clips.
  • Reading the light by lamp colour: clean red/green cycles in day and evening clips.
  • Red-light running: on sample_001 it caught a car passing a stopped queue in the kerb lane on red while pedestrians crossed.
  • Removing false people (signal heads, riders) before pedestrian rules.

Did not

  • Box-overlap accident and near-miss rules: dozens of hits per clip, all queues or cars in neighbouring lanes. Removed from Part A.
  • The first Part B scored ≈ 1.0 on most frames. Rebuilt; still unvalidated against real accidents, because the samples contain none we could find.
  • failure_to_yield is the noisiest class left: cyclists and pedestrians who cross on red trigger it.
  • No own labels for a full scored dev set before the deadline; boundaries are checked by eye.

Next

  • Label all four clips and tune thresholds per class against evaluate.py.
  • A pedestrian-signal reader, so crossing on red is no longer a car's fault.
  • Ground-plane homography from the lane markings: speeds and gaps in metres instead of pixels, which Part B needs most.
  • Fine-tune the detector on frames from this camera to remove the false "people".
